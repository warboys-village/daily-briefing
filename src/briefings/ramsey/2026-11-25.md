  <div class="news-summary">
    <p>CIL - Community Infrastructure Levy What is CIL? Community Infrastructure Levy, commonly referred to as CIL, is money collected by Huntingdonshire District Council from certain new developments. The funding is then used to help provide or improve infrastructure needed as communities grow. The money collected is broadly divided into three areas: Up to 5% can be retained by Huntingdonshire District Council towards the administration and collection of CIL. 15% is normally allocated to the Town or Parish Council for the area where the development takes place. Once an adopted Neighbourhood Plan is in place, this increases to 25%. Most of the remaining funding is retained by Huntingdonshire District Council for larger strategic infrastructure projects across the district. Ramsey Town Council currently receives the 15% neighbourhood portion. Once the Ramsey Communities Neighbourhood Plan is adopted, this will increase to 25%, giving the Council a greater share of money from qualifying development to invest back into our communities. Current projects supported or earmarked through Ramsey Town Council’s CIL funding include: Improvements at The Moorings. Contributions towards Local Highways Improvement projects. Improvements to Newtown Road Play Park. Improvements to the public toilets. New benches at the skate park. CCTV at The Moorings and New Road. The Double Yellow Lines project. The Town Council is also considering future projects, including additional parking behind the doctor’s surgery at Mews Close. Local Highways Initiative Bid (LHI): what is an LHI? The Local Highway Improvement programme allows local councils and community organisations to apply to Cambridgeshire County Council for funding towards highway improvements. Projects can include new or extended footpaths, traffic-calming measures, speed-reduction schemes, and other changes designed to improve road and pedestrian safety. Ramsey Town Council identifies potential projects and submits applications to Cambridgeshire County Council. Applications are assessed by the County Council and, if successful, funding is awarded towards the cost of the scheme. Under the current scheme, up to £25,000 can be awarded towards more complex projects, with applicants expected to contribute at least 10% of the overall cost. One recent LHI project was the extension of the footpath along Stocking Fen Road towards the industrial estate, improving pedestrian access for people travelling to and from work. The Council has also submitted an application for speed-reduction measures on the approach to Ramsey St Mary’s and is awaiting the outcome. Abbey College Road Safety Campaign Abbey College students are raising funds towards the £10,000 needed to help pay for speed reduction measures on Hollow Lane. Students have been campaigning, the school submitted an LHI bid, and speed cushions are planned. They would be grateful if you could spare the price of a coffee and scan the QR code.</p>

    <h5 class="news-title"><a href="https://www.huntspost.co.uk/news/26708649.historic-huntingdonshire-bridges-reveal-centuries-travel/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Here is some of Huntingdonshire's most unusual bridges and where to find them</a></h5>
    <div><span class="badge-status badge-other">7 Nov</span></div>
  </div>
  <div class="news-summary">
    <p>From a medieval bridge with its own chapel to a 55-arch causeway, Huntingdonshire is packed with unusual crossings. The district’s rivers, floodplains and Fenland waterways have shaped travel for centuries, leaving medieval crossings and Fen drainage bridges behind. Here are some of Huntingdonshire’s most striking bridges and where to find them. St Ives Bridge One of the county’s most extraordinary bridges spans the River Great Ouse at St Ives. The town’s first bridge was built of wood in 1107, before work began on a stone replacement in the early 15th century. The stone crossing was completed in 1425, and a chapel was added the following year. It is one of only three bridges in England with a chapel. The bridge also still bears the scars of the English Civil War. Parliamentary troops destroyed part of it to hinder Royalist forces, so a temporary drawbridge was installed. When the damaged section was rebuilt in 1716, it was given rounded arches instead of matching the pointed medieval ones, leaving a visible difference today.     <h5 class="news-title" style="color: var(--color-text-main); font-weight: 700;">Ramsey Town Council Residents Communication – Autumn 2026: CIL - Community Infrastructure Levy What is CIL? Community Infrastructure Levy, commonly referred to</h5>
    
  </div>
  <div class="news-summary" style="margin-bottom: 0;">
    <p>CIL - Community Infrastructure Levy What is CIL? Community Infrastructure Levy, commonly referred to as CIL, is money collected by Huntingdonshire District Council from certain new developments. The funding is then used to help provide or improve infrastructure needed as communities grow. The money collected is broadly divided into three areas: Up to 5% can be retained by Huntingdonshire District Council towards the administration and collection of CIL. 15% is normally allocated to the Town or Parish Council for the area where the development takes place. Once an adopted Neighbourhood Plan is in place, this increases to 25%. Most of the remaining funding is retained by Huntingdonshire District Council for larger strategic infrastructure projects across the district. Ramsey Town Council currently receives the 15% neighbourhood portion. Once the Ramsey Communities Neighbourhood Plan is adopted, this will increase to 25%, giving the Council a greater share of money from qualifying development to invest back into our communities. Current projects supported or earmarked through Ramsey Town Council’s CIL funding include: Improvements at The Moorings. Contributions towards Local Highways Improvement projects. Improvements to Newtown Road Play Park. Improvements to the public toilets. New benches at the skate park. CCTV at The Moorings and New Road. The Double Yellow Lines project. The Town Council is also considering future projects, including additional parking behind the doctor’s surgery at Mews Close. Local Highways Initiative Bid (LHI): what is an LHI? The Local Highway Improvement programme allows local councils and community organisations to apply to Cambridgeshire County Council for funding towards highway improvements. Projects can include new or extended footpaths, traffic-calming measures, speed-reduction schemes, and other changes designed to improve road and pedestrian safety. Ramsey Town Council identifies potential projects and submits applications to Cambridgeshire County Council. Applications are assessed by the County Council and, if successful, funding is awarded towards the cost of the scheme. Under the current scheme, up to £25,000 can be awarded towards more complex projects, with applicants expected to contribute at least 10% of the overall cost. One recent LHI project was the extension of the footpath along Stocking Fen Road towards the industrial estate, improving pedestrian access for people travelling to and from work. The Council has also submitted an application for speed-reduction measures on the approach to Ramsey St Mary’s and is awaiting the outcome. Abbey College Road Safety Campaign Abbey College students are raising funds towards the £10,000 needed to help pay for speed reduction measures on Hollow Lane. Students have been campaigning, the school submitted an LHI bid, and speed cushions are planned. They would be grateful if you could spare the price of a coffee and scan the QR code.</p>
